The Three Pillars

What USCIS actually cares about

You don't get EB-1A because you checked off a list of criteria. You get EB-1A because you can prove three things:

1

You are a top-end expert in your field

2

Your expertise benefits the US (national interest)

3

You will continue your work after receiving the green card

Criteria are evidence. They're the lens you use to prove the three pillars.

Meeting three EB-1A regulatory criteria is an important starting point, but it does not automatically guarantee approval. USCIS also evaluates the evidence as a whole to determine whether it demonstrates sustained national or international acclaim and extraordinary ability.

Our approach focuses on building a cohesive, well-supported case rather than simply counting criteria.

Most people chase criteria. We teach you to build the three pillars and let the criteria support that story. This is the core of everything we do, and it's where most others miss.

How This Works

The D.O.N.E. Process

Our framework is patented (US Patent No. 11,989,680 B1). We've studied over 1,000 EB-1A cases. We know what USCIS is looking for.

Define

We reverse-engineer your true expertise from your entire career. Not your title. Not your degree. Your actual professional soul. What's the common thread across all your work? What problems have you solved that most people didn't know existed? We use the DISK-WE matrix (Data, Information, Skill, Knowledge, Wisdom and Enlightenment) to extract the real expertise you have.

Organize

We build an evidence matrix showing what you've done against what the USCIS criteria require. Your attorney drafts the petition using our frameworks. We find the gaps before USCIS does. We assess criticality with the Lev and Schwartz Model, which calculates your dollar value to your employer. It is a published human-capital valuation model (Lev and Schwartz, 1971) that companies such as Infosys have used in annual reports.

Ongoing, longest phase

Nurture

We coach you to fill the gaps. Judging: journal reviews and award nominations. Writing: articles and publications. Speaking: conferences, TEDx and public engagement. Learning: going back to your fundamentals and deepening your academic grounding. Most people find evidence they never knew they had.

Final phase

Execute

We teach you how to present yourself. You draft recommendation letters using 8-10 proven frameworks, including the Steve and Lewis model, a Bayesian model for probability of evidence and the salary recommendation letter. You work with an attorney who believes in you and will advocate for you. You make informed decisions about every part of your petition.

Your title is not your expertise. Your degree is not your expertise. The common thread through every problem you've solved is your expertise. The ones who get approved are the ones who learn how to present it.

After EB-1A Approval

Adjustment of Status

Approval is a milestone, not the finish line. If your priority date is current, you file for adjustment of status.

You file Form I-485, get fingerprinted and attend an interview. Over those 6-8 months, USCIS runs a deep background check: criminal records, DUIs, traffic tickets, tax issues and financial problems.

EB-1A approval doesn't guarantee adjustment of status. If you've been straight about your background, you are in a strong position. We guide you through this phase too.

"What if I'm not a fit?"

We only take students we know we can help. We turn people down on these calls. We'd rather tell you what to work on first than take your money and set you up to fail.

"I've already started building my profile on my own."

Stop. Most people who start alone waste 6-12 months going the wrong direction. They build the wrong evidence, write articles that don't matter and chase judging USCIS doesn't care about. Then they hire us to fix it. You're not ahead. You're behind, with habits to unlearn. The Define phase shows you what matters and what wastes your time. Starting right is faster than fixing what you built wrong.

"I've already met most of my criteria. Why do I need coaching?"

Good. Then you may be exactly where coaching matters most. Having evidence and presenting evidence are two different things. You're counting criteria. We look at the case: what's your defining expertise, what connects the evidence, where are the weak spots and does it all add up to one clear story of why you're extraordinary? If your evidence is strong, we don't rebuild it. We validate it, sharpen it, connect it and position it. Don't ask how many criteria you have. Ask what conclusion your evidence leads USCIS toward.

Criteria are pieces of evidence. The petition is the argument.

"I don't have the time."

You're committing about 1 hour a day plus weekends. That's less than most people spend on social media. Now ask how much time you already spend worrying about your visa status. This is a path out of the uncertainty. You're not adding time to your life. You're getting it back. November enrollees get 8 months, so December travel doesn't cost you program time.

Analysis paralysis kills more dreams than bad decisions. You're looking at $14,500 this month, or $16,000 to $20,000 in 2027. Compare that to $800,000 or more for EB-5 or decades on EB-2.
